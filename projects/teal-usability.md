## Plan

As “customer zero”, I engaged in a workflow testing process to assess the effectiveness of Teal’s features for new users. I chose to focus primarily on the most common workflow—saving a job posting and generating a custom resume for it. Testing involved an array of job descriptions, focusing on my firsthand experience with the Teal browser extension in Microsoft Edge.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/images/projects/teal-usability/workflow.webp" alt="The Teal browser extension open beside a LinkedIn job posting for a Lead Content Designer, showing compensation, a star rating, a stage menu, top hard skills, and a Save Job button." caption="The most common workflow is saving a job posting and preparing a resume for it." %}

## Capture

I collected feedback over the course of three months regarding the usability and overall experiences with the browser extension and their website, identifying both issues and areas for improvement.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/images/projects/teal-usability/snagit-capture.webp" alt="The Snagit editor with a job description marked up: highlighted terms, a circled word, and red callouts. One callout reads, “Tool labels sentence case v. tool labels Title Case?”" caption="Snagit does a great job of capturing workflow testing results." %}

## Deliver

For simplicity, I chose to use a Figma design file to share the captured feedback. Based on previous conversations with the Teal team, I knew they were familiar with Figma and could navigate and comment on it easily as needed. Figma also enabled me to continue adding feedback as I discovered it, so it became a living document all could revisit.
